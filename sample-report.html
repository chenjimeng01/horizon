Executive summary

James and Elena Whitmore, both 40 and 38, have two children aged 7 and 5. James earns 500,000 a year; the household holds about 5m of net worth (a 2m home with a 1m repayment mortgage, and 4m of investments and cash) and enjoys a high-cost lifestyle. The goal explored here is early retirement for James at around 50, while funding private school and university for both children. The central finding is that the retirement date is not the binding constraint: the lifestyle is. On the current level of spending (about 310,000 a year of core and discretionary costs, before school fees and the mortgage), the model exhausts the investment portfolio in James's early 50s whether he stops work at 50 or keeps working to 60. When spending is moderated to about 135,000 a year of core and discretionary, retirement at 50 is funded to the end of the plan on the central assumptions, and passes a substantial estate to the children; against market variation it remains a roughly even chance, so spending flexibility in weak markets, or a few more working years, is the remaining lever. The three modelled paths below set out those trade-offs. Nothing in this report is a personal recommendation.

Security floor

The security floor asks two things. First, how much of your essential, non-discretionary spending is covered for life by guaranteed income (state pension, any defined-benefit pension, and annuities) rather than by investment returns. Second, if you chose not to sell growth assets in a downturn, how long your safe reserves (cash, NS&I and gilts) alone would cover the essentials. Growth assets are deliberately excluded from both.

Stochastic testing (Monte Carlo)

The Monte Carlo section tests the current-lifestyle plan against 1,000 randomised return paths. It meets all planned spending to the end in under 1% of those runs, with the first shortfall arriving around age 53 in the median failing run. The moderated-spending path is deterministically funded but still succeeds in only about half of the randomised paths, because it spends the portfolio close to its limit: a run of weak early markets can still exhaust it. That points to the practical shape of a robust plan: moderated spending plus flexibility to trim discretionary costs in weak markets, or a later stopping date.

Risks and structural warnings

The plan is most sensitive to spending and to investment returns. A sustained lifestyle above roughly 140,000 a year of core and discretionary spending in retirement is not supported by the current portfolio on these assumptions. School and university fees are modelled to rise with earnings. Investment returns are assumed constant in the central projection; actual returns vary, sequence matters, and capital is at risk. The estate figures assume current inheritance tax rules, including pensions entering the estate from April 2027 and the beneficiary income tax on a pension retained to a death after 75.
MPAA triggered
James: the first taxable pension withdrawal in 2043 triggers the Money Purchase Annual Allowance. From the following year, money purchase pension contributions are limited to 10,000 and carry-forward ends; contributions above that limit incur an annual allowance charge (met by the pension scheme under scheme pays by default). The rules only leave contributions unconstrained before first flexible access; tax-free-cash-only access does not trigger the Money Purchase Annual Allowance.
Lump sum allowance
James: cumulative tax-free pension elements pass the 268,275 lump sum allowance in 2044. Amounts above the allowance are in fact taxable, so the model understates tax from this point; the timing of tax-free cash is a finite resource whose use is a planning decision.

Model treatment

Annual steps. Each person is taxed individually with their own allowances, bands, NI, CGT exemption and ISA allowance; joint holdings split 50/50. Scottish non-savings rates apply where selected. Full banded UK income tax with PA taper, PSA and dividend allowance, on the 2026/27 basis with enacted Autumn 2025 Budget changes year-gated (dividend rates 10.75/35.75/39.35; savings income, and property income for taxpayers outside Scotland, at 22/42/47 from 2027/28; the salary-sacrifice NI exemption capped at 2,000 from 2029/30; thresholds frozen to 2030/31 then indexed). CGT with annual exempt amount and 18/24% banding; pensions accessed from 57 with 25% of each withdrawal tax free (cumulative tax-free elements above the 268,275 lump sum allowance are flagged, not taxed); bonds carry the 5% tax-deferred allowance with chargeable gains taxed as income; family investment companies use a two-layer treatment (company-level corporation tax, personal tax on extraction, director's loan repayments tax free); NS&I and ISA income is taxed at destination rates in non-UK years; the temporary non-residence rule charges gains realised during absences of five complete tax years or less on return. Gilt ladders hold each rung to redemption: coupons are savings income, redemption proceeds are free of capital gains tax (gilts are exempt) and values accrete toward par; rungs can be sold early without tax and ladder cashflows are never shocked in simulations. Index-linked rungs are entered in today's money and uplift with inflation (the replayed CPI path in backtests, the assumed rate otherwise). The security floor reports the years of baseline (non-discretionary) spending fundable from secure sources alone - state pension, annuity and DB income taxed as if it were the only income, plus cash, NS&I and gilt ladders - with gifts and one-off purchases excluded. Where assets or salaries carry a concentration group, the share of the extended balance sheet (asset values plus the present value of future earnings, in today's money, life events ignored) in each group is measured and warned on above 40%. A disposal marked MVL winds a company up with capital treatment: corporation tax on company gains, the director's loan repaid tax free, and the distribution taxed as gains, with Business Asset Disposal Relief inside the 1m lifetime limit when claimed (18% from April 2026, 14% before; trading-status and anti-phoenixing conditions are flagged, not modelled). Where enabled, bed-and-ISA moves GIA value into unused ISA allowance each UK year (CGT on the move paid from proceeds) and CGT harvesting uplifts GIA cost basis inside the unused annual exemption (the 30-day share-matching rule is an execution caveat: repurchase a similar, not identical, holding). Spending guardrails, where applied in simulations, cut discretionary spending when the portfolio falls below the stated share of the central plan and re-test annually. Each person's plan age is their modelled death age: the plan runs to the second death, the first death passes everything to the survivor (spouse exemption) with incomes and state pension stopping and spending scaled by the survivor-spending assumption. Inheritance tax is computed each year on a second-death basis (transferable nil-rate bands, residence band with taper, pensions in the estate from April 2027). A pension retained to a second death at 75 or later is additionally taxed as the beneficiary's income on drawdown, on top of that inheritance tax; this pension death-tax is shown separately, assumes a beneficiary marginal rate of 40% (a stated assumption) and treats the pension as the estate's top slice. A death before 75 leaves inherited pensions free of that income tax. Lifetime gifts use the annual exemptions and join the figures as potentially exempt transfers with seven-year clocks, nil-rate-band consumption in gift order and taper relief, and a charitable legacy share applies the 36% rate when it reaches 10% of the baseline. Liabilities are interest-only or amortising repayment loans. Life events scale or pause salaries; planned disposals sell assets or fractions of them by wrapper rules. Pension contributions above the annual allowance are flagged, not charged. Bands indexed 2% from 2030.
