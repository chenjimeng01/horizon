Executive summary

James and Elena Whitmore, both 40 and 38, have two children aged 7 and 5. James earns 500,000 a year; the household holds about 5m of net worth (a 2m home with a 1m mortgage, and 4m of investments and cash) and enjoys a high-cost lifestyle. The goal explored here is early retirement for James at around 50, while funding private school and university for both children. The central finding is that the retirement date is not the binding constraint: the lifestyle is. On the current level of spending (about 310,000 a year of core and discretionary costs, before school fees and the mortgage), the model exhausts the investment portfolio in James's early 60s whether he stops work at 50 or keeps working to 60. When spending is moderated to about 160,000 a year of core and discretionary, retirement at 50 is fully funded to the end of the plan and still leaves a substantial estate to the children. The three modelled paths below set out those trade-offs. Nothing in this report is a personal recommendation.

Scenarios compared

Three paths are modelled on the same assumptions. Retire at 50 on the current lifestyle: sustained spending of about 310,000 a year exceeds what the portfolio can support after James stops earning, and the model shows the investment portfolio exhausted around age 60, after which the lifestyle could only be maintained by drawing on the home. Keep the lifestyle and work to 60: the point of exhaustion moves later, to around age 75, but does not go away, because the lifestyle exceeds net earnings even while James works, so the extra years add less than they might. Retire at 50 with spending moderated to about 160,000 a year of core and discretionary: this path is funded to the end of the plan, and passes an estate of roughly 10.7m to the children after inheritance tax and the pension death-tax. The difference between the paths is spending, not the retirement age.

Stochastic testing (Monte Carlo)

The Monte Carlo section tests the current-lifestyle plan against 1,000 randomised return paths. It meets all planned spending to the end in about 3% of those runs, with the first shortfall arriving around age 59 in the median failing run. This confirms the deterministic result: the current lifestyle is not resilient to ordinary market variation. The moderated-spending path is materially more robust, because its spending sits within what the portfolio can sustain.

Risks and structural warnings

The plan is most sensitive to spending and to investment returns. A sustained lifestyle above roughly 170,000 a year of core and discretionary spending in retirement is not supported by the current portfolio on these assumptions. A Money Purchase Annual Allowance flag arises when James first draws taxable pension income, which would constrain further pension funding from the following year. School and university fees are modelled to rise with earnings. Investment returns are assumed constant at the stated rates; actual returns vary, sequence matters, and capital is at risk. The estate figures assume current inheritance tax rules, including pensions entering the estate from April 2027 and the beneficiary income tax on a pension retained to a death after 75.
MPAA triggered
James: the first taxable pension withdrawal in 2043 triggers the Money Purchase Annual Allowance. From the following year, money purchase pension contributions above 10,000 are flagged (the charge itself is not modelled) and carry-forward ends. If continued funding were wanted, the rules only leave contributions unconstrained before first flexible access; tax-free-cash-only access does not trigger the MPAA.
Lump sum allowance
James: cumulative tax-free pension elements pass the 268,275 lump sum allowance in 2044. Amounts above the allowance are in fact taxable, so the model understates tax from this point; the timing of tax-free cash is a finite resource whose use is a planning decision.

Model treatment

Annual steps. Each person is taxed individually with their own allowances, bands, NI, CGT exemption and ISA allowance; joint holdings split 50/50. Scottish non-savings rates apply where selected. Full banded UK income tax with PA taper, PSA and dividend allowance, on the 2026/27 basis with enacted Autumn 2025 Budget changes year-gated (dividend rates 10.75/35.75/39.35; savings and rUK property income at 22/42/47 from 2027/28; the salary-sacrifice NI exemption capped at 2,000 from 2029/30; thresholds frozen to 2030/31 then indexed). CGT with annual exempt amount and 18/24% banding; pensions accessed from 57 with 25% of each withdrawal tax free (cumulative tax-free elements above the 268,275 lump sum allowance are flagged, not taxed); bonds carry the 5% tax-deferred allowance with chargeable gains taxed as income; family investment companies use a two-layer treatment (company-level corporation tax, personal tax on extraction, director's loan repayments tax free); NS&I and ISA income is taxed at destination rates in non-UK years; the temporary non-residence rule charges gains realised during absences of five complete tax years or less on return. Gilt ladders hold each rung to redemption: coupons are savings income, redemption proceeds are free of capital gains tax (gilts are exempt) and values accrete toward par; rungs can be sold early without tax and ladder cashflows are never shocked in simulations. Index-linked rungs are entered in today's money and uplift with inflation (the replayed CPI path in backtests, the assumed rate otherwise). The security floor reports the years of baseline (non-discretionary) spending fundable from secure sources alone - state pension, annuity and DB income taxed as if it were the only income, plus cash, NS&I and gilt ladders - with gifts and one-off purchases excluded. Where assets or salaries carry a concentration group, the share of the extended balance sheet (asset values plus the present value of future earnings, in today's money, life events ignored) in each group is measured and warned on above 40%. A disposal marked MVL winds a company up with capital treatment: corporation tax on company gains, the director's loan repaid tax free, and the distribution taxed as gains, with Business Asset Disposal Relief inside the 1m lifetime limit when claimed (18% from April 2026, 14% before; trading-status and anti-phoenixing conditions are flagged, not modelled). Where enabled, bed-and-ISA moves GIA value into unused ISA allowance each UK year (CGT on the move paid from proceeds) and CGT harvesting uplifts GIA cost basis inside the unused annual exemption (the 30-day share-matching rule is an execution caveat: repurchase a similar, not identical, holding). Spending guardrails, where applied in simulations, cut discretionary spending when the portfolio falls below the stated share of the central plan and re-test annually. Each person's plan age is their modelled death age: the plan runs to the second death, the first death passes everything to the survivor (spouse exemption) with incomes and state pension stopping and spending scaled by the survivor-spending assumption. Inheritance tax is computed each year on a second-death basis (transferable nil-rate bands, residence band with taper, pensions in the estate from April 2027). A pension retained to a second death at 75 or later is additionally taxed as the beneficiary's income on drawdown, on top of that inheritance tax; this pension death-tax is shown separately, assumes a beneficiary marginal rate of 40% (a stated assumption) and treats the pension as the estate's top slice. A death before 75 leaves inherited pensions free of that income tax. Lifetime gifts use the annual exemptions and join the figures as potentially exempt transfers with seven-year clocks, nil-rate-band consumption in gift order and taper relief, and a charitable legacy share applies the 36% rate when it reaches 10% of the baseline. Liabilities are interest-only or amortising repayment loans. Life events scale or pause salaries; planned disposals sell assets or fractions of them by wrapper rules. Pension contributions above the annual allowance are flagged, not charged. Bands indexed 2% from 2030.

Model coverage (generated from the engine, always complete)

The model covers: per-person banded UK income tax with personal-allowance taper, personal savings and dividend allowances, and Scottish non-savings rates where selected; enacted Autumn 2025 Budget changes, year-gated to their start dates; capital gains tax with the annual exempt amount and 18/24% banding; ISA including bed-and-ISA and CGT harvesting; onshore and offshore bonds with the 5% deferred allowance and chargeable gains taxed as income; NS&I and gilt ladders, both fixed and index-linked; pensions accessed from 57 with 25% tax free per withdrawal and a lump sum allowance flag; the pension annual allowance and money purchase annual allowance, flagged; inherited-pension income tax on a pre- or post-75 death; family investment companies on a two-layer basis (corporation tax then personal tax on extraction); the temporary non-residence five-year clawback and a residency timeline with jurisdiction rates; the security floor: years of baseline spending fundable from secure income and liquid reserves; concentration across the extended balance sheet including the present value of future earnings, warned above 40%; members voluntary liquidation with Business Asset Disposal Relief inside the lifetime limit; mortality, the second-death transition with spouse exemption, and survivor-spending scaling; inheritance tax with transferable nil-rate bands, the residence band and taper, and pensions in the estate from April 2027; the pension death-tax on a pension retained to a post-75 second death; lifetime gifts as potentially exempt transfers with seven-year taper and nil-rate-band ordering, and a charitable-legacy 36% rate; liabilities on an interest-only or amortising basis, life events that scale or pause salaries, and planned disposals by wrapper rules; Monte Carlo and a historical backtest, with optional spending guardrails.

Not modelled: DB transfer values, trusts and CLTs, gifts with reservation, corporate exit charges other than s185, departure exit taxes, FX.
